    Overlay key and interpretation

    Coloured prediction outlines: long dashes for an assigned weak maximum, whether the fit passes or fails the screen; otherwise short dashes for below-threshold fits. Passing fits use solid outlines for strong matches and dotted outlines when no maximum is assigned. Faint outlines mark positions that cannot be checked reliably. Separate white weak-detection markers sit at the observed positions; their shape can be changed or hidden. Purple and teal outlines are primary and secondary geometry-only grids, with no phase assigned. Marker shapes are selectable independently. The separate geometry check uses freely fitted reciprocal grids and different acceptance rules. Showing a fit does not accept it or change its phase label.

    Ambiguous vs two candidate structures

    Phase ambiguous: Passing fits of different structures explain the same or largely overlapping peaks. They are competing explanations. Their ranks are close, or the leading fit to exactly the same profiles uses extra scale freedom or has more unsupported predictions.

    Two candidate structures: Passing fits of different structures explain separate sets of peaks. Each pattern has its own evidence, so both may contribute.

    Multiple orientations: Separate peak sets support passing fits of the same structure at different orientations.

    A separate pattern must add at least 3 original strong profiles not matched by the leading fit, account for more than 15% of the total weighted peak evidence, and span nonparallel directions. Additional weak maxima do not count toward this check. Neither fit needs 50% peak coverage.

    Ambiguous does not mean a mixture. Two candidate structures suggests separate contributions, but does not confirm two phases or their proportions. The geometry-only check is a separate check of periodicity without assigning a structure.

      Analysis settings

      Reference unit cells are set under Material above. Fixed modes use the same selected scale in every region. Automatic modes fit each candidate independently in every region within reciprocal scale multipliers 0.85–1.15. Model a = reference a / fitted a* scale, and model c = reference c / fitted c* scale. Automatic fits use a soft scale penalty: strength × mean[((multiplier − 1) / 0.05)²]. At the default strength 0.10, a uniform 5% adjustment costs 0.10 score and 10% costs 0.40. HCP a* and c* costs are averaged, so a uniform change is not charged twice. Zero disables the penalty; fixed comparison modes are not penalized. This is an adjustable preference for smaller changes, not a calibrated strain limit. It acts before choosing the best zone axis; rank score must still exceed 0.20. The two-scale HCP modes retain extra flexibility. Overall scale + HCP/ω c/a uses s = √(a* scale × c* scale) and r = (c/a)/(reference c/a) = a* scale / c* scale. Its scale cost is λ[ln(s)/ln(1.05)]² + μ[ln(r)/ln(1.05)]². Both weights default to 0.10 and are independently adjustable. Cubic candidates pay only the overall term; HCP zones insensitive to c pay only for their observed a scale. The original per-axis bounds remain, with no extra tight c/a cap. This reparameterizes the same positional fits; penalties rank the explored fits rather than moving them toward the reference. If two accepted structures explain exactly the same profiles, a positional-score leader that needs more fitted scale parameters or more unsupported predictions remains ambiguous. Scale comparison is available for all images and remembered across images in this browser. It resizes model predictions; measured reciprocal axes use the image calibration. For direct diffraction, the centre and reciprocal calibration can be edited above. A better fit after resizing does not establish lattice strain or confirm a phase. Peak coverage is the matched share of the total weight of original detected peak profiles. FFT weights depend on height above the radial background; direct-spot weights use compressed background-subtracted integrated intensity, with local contrast retained for unreliable measurements. Both are capped at 1–6. It is not an intensity fraction, phase fraction or probability. Peaks S+W / all separates original strong profiles from additional weak maxima. Prediction-site checks use a search radius from the window width (FFT) or measured spot width (diffraction), at least 0.10 nm⁻¹, local background, and rotated control positions. Weak-peak bonus (0–0.5) and Missing-peak penalty (0–2) are independent. Each defaults to 0.10; zero disables only its own term. A strong match has no missing cost, an unsupported prediction has full cost, and a weak match retains 50–100% of that cost according to its strength and positional agreement. Unchecked positions are excluded. Missing half-weight controls radial attenuation of the cost, with zero meaning no attenuation. Weak maxima do not count toward the four-profile screen or separate-pattern criteria. Missing reflections can be suppressed by image formation; these are exploratory ranking weights, not probabilities.

      Exploratory HCP/BCC/FCC comparison using a finite library of templates from a limited set of zone axes. Untested orientations and other structures can affect the result; candidate labels are not confirmed phase identities.

      Analysis settings
      Individual positive/negative features remain available. Combinations use the mean plus signed, score-weighted selected PCs at every sampled window. Together fits one selected combination; all subsets fits every group of two or more selected PCs. For 8 PCs this is 247 combinations per window and can take much longer. Retained candidates are checked against every original unbroadened window. Use the checkbox on each signal card to include it in fitting. All computed signals remain visible. Changing these choices requires refitting, not recomputing PCA.
      PCA contrastPer signal enhances each component separately. Shared across signals uses one symmetric, zero-centred map-score range across all computed PCs.       Mapping

      New + improved windows counts both kinds of benefit equally. Improved windows first prioritizes higher scores in already-supported windows, then new support. For example, 10 new + 2 improved beats 0 new + 5 improved in the first sort; the second sort prefers the 5 improvements. Passing windows sorts by total passing count, then mean passing score. Cumulative passing counts unique windows covered by this row and all rows above it. New and Better are that row’s increments, independent of ticks. All percentages use all checked windows; Better counts any strictly higher local score, not its size. Equal scores and failed fits do not count. Select top ticks the first N; use checkboxes for a custom pool or click a name to show only it. Sorting preserves custom selections. The map summary reports the actual ticked pool.

      Mean spectra and interpretation

      PCA components describe signed differences from the mean. NMF components are nonnegative spectral factors; neither is a crystal-phase assignment. One distorted lattice can occupy several components; two phases can share one. Group activity combines selected score magnitudes without aligning crystals. Smoothing may hide nearby reflections. Compare the original spectra and rerun with smoothing off before interpreting a boundary. Overlapping windows are correlated.

      Sampling uses full square physical windows across this image, ignoring the current ROI/polygon. Decomposition does not use detector thresholds or crystal fitting. Component indexing uses captured material and scale settings plus its own relative peak cutoff. PCA fits positive and negative deviations separately and checks every original window without a score-sign gate. Colours and map values are not phase fractions.

      Inspired by Jany, Janas & Krok (2020). Includes PCA, Euclidean NMF and exploratory indexing of component combinations. NMF signal shares are not explained variance or phase fractions; a low reconstruction error does not establish correct separation.
